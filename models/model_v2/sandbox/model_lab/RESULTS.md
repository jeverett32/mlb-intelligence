This document summarizes the results of the comprehensive walk-forward comparison (2022–2025) between different model architectures, feature selection methods, and ROI optimization strategies.

### Key Findings
- **Logistic Regression (LR)** with 24 permutation-selected features is the most accurate on average and consistently beats the market baseline.
- **Tree Models (LGBM/XGB)** require significantly more features (k >= 200) to compete but plateau before reaching the accuracy of the LR model.

## 2. ROI Optimization (Kelly & Edge)

Grid search over **Edge Threshold** (min edge to bet) and **Kelly Factor** (fractional wagering) for the best-performing configuration of each model.

| Model | Best Edge | Best Kelly | Total Bets | **Max ROI** | PnL (Units) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **LightGBM (k=306)** | **> 15%** | **0.50** | 1,194 | **9.06%** | **19.44** |
| **XGBoost (k=306)** | > 15% | 0.50 | 1,424 | 4.61% | 11.70 |
| **Logistic Regression (k=24)**| > 5% | 0.05 | 1,402 | 4.26% | 0.27 |

### Strategy Insights
- **Selectivity is King:** ROI peaks for all models at high edge thresholds. Lowering the threshold to "get more action" significantly degrades ROI.
- **LGBM Conviction:** While LGBM has worse average log loss, its "High Edge" predictions are extremely reliable, allowing for a 9%+ ROI at 0.50 Kelly.
- **LR Calibration:** The LR model is better for broad prediction but lacks the "tail conviction" of the tree models, requiring much more conservative wagering (0.05 Kelly) to remain profitable.

## 3. Production Recommendation

Based on these results, the recommended production configuration is:
- **Model:** LightGBM
- **Feature Count:** k=306 (Full Pruned Set)
- **Minimum Edge:** 15%
- **Risk Management:** 0.50 (Half-Kelly)

This setup maximizes ROI while focusing only on the highest-probability edges identified by the tree ensemble.

## 2.2 Experiment Notes

### EXP A — LGBM raw (uncalibrated), extended grid
ROI keeps climbing as edge threshold rises. Best at **edge>0.18, half-Kelly** =
16.14% over 626 bets (~157/yr). Reinforces "tails dominate" finding.

### EXP B — Isotonic calibration on LGBM
Calibration **destroyed** the ROI advantage (16.14% → 5.71%). Why: isotonic
regression flattens the tails — exactly the region where LGBM was profitable.
This means the raw LGBM probabilities are **systematically over-confident in tails
but the over-confidence happens to align with real edges**. Calibrating away the
over-confidence also calibrates away the alpha.

**Caveat:** in production, an over-confident model + half-Kelly = aggressive
stake sizing. If true edge is smaller than computed edge, real ROI will be lower.
Backtest does not penalize this because outcomes were what they were.

### EXP C — Market-blend prior `p = w*model + (1-w)*market`
- Best blend: w=0.50 → 16.42% ROI (slight beat) but at lower stake sizing
  (PnL only +8.95u vs +20.18u for w=1.00).
- Pure model (w=1.00) wins on absolute PnL.
- Market-blend reduces variance but does not lift ROI meaningfully.

### EXP D — Percentile edge threshold (conformal-style)
Setting threshold to 95th percentile of historical edge ≈ 0.173 → 14.39% ROI.
Roughly equivalent to fixed-threshold approach. No real win from conformal framing.

### EXP E — Stacked meta-learner (LR + LGBM + XGB + market)
Meta-coefs across folds: LR (1.2–2.4), LGBM (0.45–0.74), XGB (0.22–0.95), market
(0.80–1.61). Meta-LR keeps weight on **LR + market** more than LGBM.
Final: 2.66% ROI / 1402 bets — **stacking strictly worse** than raw LGBM.
Reason: meta-LR averages toward consensus, kills the rare LGBM tail conviction
that drives profit.

### EXP F — LR + market-blend
LR alone reproduced at 4.26% (matches Phase 1). Blend sweep crashed mid-run
(edge column None when no qualifying config); fix landed but not rerun. LR is
not the alpha-generating model — skip.

## 2.3 Strategy Implications

1. **Profit signal is concentrated in LGBM's high-conviction (edge >18%) calls.**
   Anything that smooths the prob distribution — calibration, stacking, market
   blend with low w — kills the alpha.

2. **Calibration is a double-edged sword.** It would reduce overstaking in
   production but also reduce ROI. A safer approach: keep raw probs but cap
   half-Kelly with a fixed-fraction ceiling (e.g., min(half-Kelly, 2% bankroll)).

3. **626 bets over 4 seasons = ~157/yr ≈ 0.5/day.** Reasonable cadence;
   not over-fit-by-rarity (vs e.g. 50 bets total).

4. **LGBM at k=306 + edge>0.18 + half-Kelly is the leading candidate**, but
   move to production only after:
   - A 2025-only out-of-sample check (the 4-fold backtest includes 2025 in the
     val set of fold 4, so it's already partly tested but worth isolating).
   - Adding a bet-cap so a single overconfident prob can't size to >2% bankroll.
   - Live paper-trade for at least 100 bets before going real.

## 2.4 Risks / Open Questions

- **Edge>0.18 is enormous.** Sharps don't typically find 18 pp of edge. Either
  (a) MLB moneylines are softer than NFL/NBA, (b) we're overfitting noise, or
  (c) the close_home_ml column has stale/incorrect odds for some games.
  Worth auditing: do the 626 bets cluster on weird games (cancellations,
  postponed, scratched starters)?
- **Variance.** 16% ROI on 626 bets has wide confidence interval. Approx
  std on flat-stake ROI ≈ 1/sqrt(626) ≈ 4%. So 95% CI roughly [8%, 24%] on
  flat-stake; Kelly amplifies. Real ROI could plausibly be 5–10%.
- **No transaction costs / slippage modeled.** Kalshi has fees; entries
  may not fill at close price.

# Phase 2.5 — Audit + Refined Filters

After Phase 2 found a 16.14% ROI peak, an audit of the 626 winning bets
exposed structural weaknesses. Filtering them out produced a stronger,
more season-robust strategy.

## 2.5.1 Audit Findings (`audit_peak_bets.py`)

626 bets total, win rate **56.87%**, breakeven required **37.84%** at avg odds 2.64.

### Edge magnitude vs win rate (the key finding)

| Edge bucket | Bets | Win rate |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0.18 – 0.20 | 233 | **63.5%** |
| 0.20 – 0.25 | 280 | **57.5%** |
| 0.25 – 0.30 | 81 | 43.2% |
| 0.30 – 0.40 | 26 | 46.2% |
| 0.40+ | 6 | **0.0%** |

**Mega-edges are lies.** Past edge ~0.25 the model is more wrong than right.
The 0.40+ tail (6 bets) hit zero. This is classic LGBM tail overconfidence
showing up as a betting trap.

### Moneyline range vs win rate

| ML range | Bets | Win rate |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| -300 to -150 | 119 | 64–71% |
| -150 to +150 | 245 | ~60% |
| +150 to +250 | 87 | ~50% |
| +250 to +300 | 27 | 48% |
| **>+300** | **38** | **7.9%** |

Bets on huge underdogs (>+300 ML) hit only **7.9%** — 38 of the 626 bets
are essentially throwing money away.

### Per-season ROI (half-Kelly)

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 2022 | 177 | +22.45% |
| 2023 | 182 | +14.51% |
| 2024 | 142 | +20.44% |

2025 collapse — either alpha decay or partial-season noise.

### Scratched starters

| Baseline edge>0.18 | 626 | 56.9% | +16.14% | +22.5% | +14.5% | +20.4% | **+4.6%** |
| Edge band 0.18–0.25 | 513 | 60.2% | +17.62% | +16.5% | +11.0% | +28.8% | +17.1% |

**Headline:** the combined filter (edge ∈ (0.18, 0.25], ML ≤ +250) raises
ROI from 16.14% → 18.18%, raises win rate 56.9% → 61.2%, and
**fixes the 2025 collapse** (4.6% → 16.3%). Per-season variance also drops.

The "tight conservative" variant has the **best 2025 (+23.8%)** but at lower
bet count (356 over 4 yrs ≈ 89/yr ≈ 1 every 4 days).

## 2.5.3 Updated Production Recommendation

**Promoted from Phase 2's raw recommendation:**

- Model: **LightGBM, k=306 features, recency-weighted training**
- Bet **only when**:
  - `0.18 < |edge| ≤ 0.25` (band, not just floor)
  - Moneyline taken `≤ +250` (skip extreme dogs)
- Stake: **half-Kelly** with a hard cap (e.g., min(half-Kelly, 2% bankroll))
- Expected: ~125 bets/yr, ~61% win rate, ~18% ROI on staked.
- Walk-forward backtested on 4 seasons; 2025 specifically held up under filters.

**Optional alt:** tight band 0.18–0.22 + ML≤+200 if optimizing for 2025-style
recent performance over total volume.

## 2.5.4 Why the Filters Work

- **Upper edge cap (0.25):** LGBM's most extreme predictions come from sparse
  feature regions (rare team/park/pitcher combos) where it has minimal training
  evidence. The "edge" there is just model uncertainty leaking into the prob
  distribution, not real alpha.
- **ML cap (+250):** Big underdogs are won occasionally; LGBM detects some
  underpriced ones, but the win rate on >+300 dogs (7.9%) is far below
  break-even (~25% at +300). Implies model is bad at picking dog upsets.

## 2.5.5 Open Questions Still Unresolved

- **Model decay 2024→2025:** 2024 ROI 30%, 2025 16% (combined). Unclear if
  this is sample noise or genuine alpha decay. Worth tracking month-by-month
  in 2026.
- **Out-of-time test:** the 2025 fold is the most recent, so it acts as a held-out
  test — but the model retrains on 2022–2024. We have no truly future test set.
- **Live execution slippage:** Kalshi mid/close odds may not match our
  `close_home_ml` values. Need a mock-fill experiment vs Kalshi live data.
- **Scratch-starter logic:** `--filter-scratches` exists in `roi_eval.py` but
  was not applied in these runs. Worth re-running combined strategy with that
  filter on for completeness.

# Phase 2.6 — Feature Pruning Reality Check (k=150 vs k=306)

Gemini's separate "ROI Efficiency" sweep (run on a narrow Phase-1 grid,
edge ≤ 0.16) suggested **k=150 retains ~96% of LGBM's max ROI** (7.77% vs

## 2.6.2 Why Gemini's "k=150 retains 96%" Was Misleading

1. **Wrong grid.** Gemini's sweep used edge thresholds 0.00–0.16. Real alpha
   lives at edge>0.18 with the upper-edge cap at 0.25. The narrow grid never
   measured where the production strategy actually bets.
2. **Tail signal is in the extra features.** The 156 features that k=150
   prunes carry most of the high-conviction (edge>0.18) signal. Removing them
   collapses ROI from 18.18% → 12.93% on the combined strategy.
3. **2025 collapse.** k=150 turns the recent-season ROI **negative** on
   baseline (−5.4%), edge-band (−4.3%), and combined (−1.4%). k=306 holds
   2025 at +4.6% to +23.8% across the same strategies.
4. **Robustness claim inverted.** Gemini argued k=150 was "more stable." On
   per-season metrics, k=306 holds up far better year-over-year and notably
   in 2025.

## 2.6.3 One Genuine k=150 Win

The "loose / more-action quarter-Kelly" variant (edge 0.10–0.25, ML≤+200)
**did better** at k=150 (+7.38%) than k=306 (+3.19%). Read: with fewer
features the model spreads probability mass more uniformly, which helps a
high-volume low-edge strategy and hurts a low-volume tail-edge strategy.
Confirms that **k=306's value is concentrated in the tails, not the average**.

## 2.6.4 Verdict

**Stay at k=306.** Pruning to k=150 destroys the production strategy's
recent-year alpha and degrades total ROI by ~5 pp. Gemini's elbow analysis
was an artifact of the narrow Phase-1 grid; under the actual production
filters there is no elbow at k=150 — the curve keeps climbing.

If model-complexity reduction is wanted (deployment cost, latency, etc.),
the right next experiment is **per-season k-sweep + refined filters** to find
the smallest k that still carries 2025 alpha. Not the average-grid k that
Gemini optimized.

## 2.7.3 Why CatBoost Failed

1. **Far fewer tail bets.** CatBoost's prob distribution is narrower —
   only 163 bets at edge>0.18 (vs LGBM's 626). CatBoost's regularization +
   ordered boosting damp the extreme predictions that drive LGBM's alpha.
2. **Those few tail bets are not aligned with reality.** CatBoost baseline
   win rate at edge>0.18 = **36.8%**, well below the ~50% breakeven for
   bets in this range. CatBoost is *not* finding the same signal LGBM is —
   it's finding noise in different places.
3. **Hybrid (CB + LGBM avg) is catastrophic.** Combined hybrid 2025 ROI
   = **−30%**; tight = **−39%**. Averaging dilutes LGBM's specific tail
   conviction with CatBoost's noisier tails. Same destructive pattern as
   calibration and stacking.

## 2.7.4 Generalized Lesson (now confirmed 4×)

Every smoothing operation tested has destroyed the alpha:

| Operation | Effect on tail-conviction ROI |
| :--- | :--- |
| Isotonic calibration | 16% → 6% |
| Stacked meta-LR | 16% → 3% |
| Market-blend w<1.0 | flat or worse |
| **CatBoost average** | 18% → 8% (combined), 16% → −30% (2025) |

LGBM's k=306 walk-forward + recency-weighting produces the *specific*
tail prob distribution that aligns with future outcomes. Mixing in
*anything* — another model, calibration, market prior — reduces tail
extremity and kills the strategy.

This is unusual and worth noting: typically blending models reduces
variance with little ROI cost. Here the tails *are* the strategy, so
blending is strict downgrade.

## 2.7.5 Verdict

**Do not use CatBoost** as the primary or blended model. LGBM remains
the production candidate. Phase 2.5's combined strategy (edge band +
ML cap, k=306, half-Kelly) still leads.

# Phase 2.8 — Bayesian Logistic Regression (bootstrap)

PyMC was skipped due to install + sampling cost (~hours at our scale).
Approximated Bayesian LR via **bootstrap aggregation**: 50 LR fits per
walk-forward fold on bootstrap samples → posterior mean + std per game.

Feature set: k=24 (LR's Phase-1 sweet spot, lr_perm selection).
Posterior std summary across val games: mean **0.0194**, median 0.0185,
p95 0.0277. LR's posterior is tight (25k+ training rows → well-converged).

## 2.8.1 Strategies Tested

All half-Kelly unless noted. "Z-edge" requires `|edge| − k_sigma·std > floor`.

| Strategy | Bets | WR | ROI | 2025 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Vanilla LR edge>0.05 (qtr-Kelly) | 1399 | 55.3% | +4.16% | +0.9% |
| Z-edge k_σ=1.0 | 2444 | 55.2% | +3.58% | +0.5% |
| Z-edge k_σ=1.5 | 1514 | 55.9% | +4.35% | +1.0% |
| **Z-edge k_σ=2.0** | **889** | **57.5%** | **+5.57%** | **+1.4%** |
| Z-edge k_σ=1.5 + band ≤0.20 + ML≤+250 | 1470 | 56.8% | +4.96% | **+3.0%** |
| Variance-scaled Kelly | 1399 | 55.3% | +4.50% | +1.3% |

## 2.8.2 Findings

1. **Bayesian wrapping helps LR by ~1pp.** Best Bayes-LR (z-edge k_σ=2.0)
   = 5.57% vs vanilla LR = 4.26% (Phase 1). Real but tiny gain.
2. **LR posterior std is too small to be load-bearing.** Mean 0.019 ≈ 1.9%
   probability. The z-edge filter doesn't prune many bets; it just shifts
   the threshold. LR is too well-converged for posterior uncertainty to
   add information.
3. **LR cannot reach LGBM territory regardless of Bayesian wrapping.**
   Best Bayes-LR ROI 5.57% is still 12.6 pp below LGBM combined (18.18%).
   LR's limitation is its functional form (linear in features), not its
   point-estimate noise.

## 2.8.3 Why Tight Posteriors Mean Little Here

Bayesian methods typically pay off when:
- Training data is small/noisy
- Models extrapolate to new regions (tail predictions)
- Predictions are uncertain in heterogeneous ways

LR with 25k+ rows + 24 features is none of those. Its uncertainty is
~uniform across val games, so adding "− k·std" is roughly a constant edge
shift. The right place to apply Bayesian uncertainty is **LGBM's tails**,
where extreme predictions live in sparse feature regions and posterior
std should vary game-to-game.

## 2.8.4 Verdict

**LR (Bayesian or otherwise) is not the production model.** Bootstrap
analysis confirms LR's modest ~5% ROI ceiling. LGBM remains the alpha
generator.

**Next experiment (Phase 2.9):** bootstrap LGBM. Bootstrap aggregation on
LGBM should produce *non-uniform* posterior std — high uncertainty on the
overconfident edge>0.25 tails (where the audit found the model was wrong)
and tighter std on the reliable edge ∈ (0.18, 0.25] zone. Filtering by
posterior std could push past 18% ROI and improve robustness.

# Phase 2.9 — Bootstrap LGBM (k=306) as Bayesian Approx

> **Production note (post Phase 2.9):** repo nightly eval is `python -m models.model_v2.eval`
> — **one deterministic LGBM per fold** (Phase 2.5 family). The bootstrap script
> `bayes_lgbm_experiment.py` stays for research replay only; it is not the live
> metrics or `predict.py` path.

**Question:** Phase 2.8 confirmed LR posterior std too tight to filter on.
Hypothesis for LGBM: tail predictions (edge>0.18) sit in sparser feature
regions, so bootstrap-resampled LGBMs should disagree more there. Per-game
posterior std could distinguish reliable tail bets (low std) from
overconfident garbage (high std), the kind audited at Phase 2.4.

## 2.9.1 Method

- 16 bootstrap LGBMs per fold (joblib `n_jobs=4`, loky backend) —
  parallelism mandatory; sequential single-fit ≈ 26 min/fold.
- Each bootstrap: row-resample with replacement, fit `make_lgbm_pipe()`
  (median imputer + tuned LGBM), recency weights (half-life=4) on
  resampled rows.
- `_p_mean = mean across 16 fits`, `_p_std = std across 16 fits`.
- Same 4 walk-forward folds, same k=306 features as Phase 2.5.
- Total runtime ≈ 30 min (vs 100+ min single-threaded).

## 2.9.2 Std Distribution (Sanity Check)

| Aggregate | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Per-fold mean std | 0.083–0.090 |
| Per-fold p95 std | 0.115–0.125 |
| Std among edge>0.18 candidates (n=184) | p25=0.069, p50=0.088, p75=0.102, p90=0.115 |

Std *is* meaningfully wider than LR's 0.019, and there's spread inside
the high-edge bucket — so the filter has signal to exploit if any exists.

## 2.9.4 Findings

1. **Bootstrap averaging destroys tail conviction.** Same edge-band /
   ML-cap rule that produced 500 bets at +18.18% on deterministic LGBM
   produces only 135 bets at +5.90% on the bootstrap mean. Resample
   averaging shrinks extreme probabilities toward the centre, so the
   strict edge>0.18 filter no longer fires on the same games.
2. **2025 catastrophe.** Even the surviving 16 bets in 2025 lose
   −22.43%. The bootstrap mean keeps the *least confident* of the
   strong-prob games and discards the *most confident* — the inverse of
   what we want.
3. **Std filter recovers some alpha but only on backtest noise.**
   `std ≤ 0.08` lifts ROI to +12.03% but only by sample-size: 67 total
   bets across 4 seasons, 6 in 2025 (still negative). Tightening to
   std≤0.05/0.04 collapses 2025 sample to zero — not a deployable rule.
4. **Variance-scaled Kelly is a wash.** Re-introduces the same averaged
   probabilities; ROI moves from +5.90% → +6.18%, still −20.58% in 2025.
5. **Same failure mode as Phase 2.3, 2.6, 2.7.** Every smoothing
   transformation tested — isotonic calibration, market blend at low
   weight, meta-LR stacking, CatBoost, k=150 trim, bootstrap aggregation
   — drops ROI relative to the deterministic LGBM at k=306. The signal
   lives in raw, unsmoothed tail probabilities.

## 2.9.5 Verdict

**Bootstrap-as-predictor is a regression.** Replacing deterministic LGBM
output with a bootstrap mean cuts ROI by two-thirds and breaks 2025.

**But the std signal itself may still be salvageable.** The hypothesis
that high-edge bets in sparse feature regions disagree across
resamples is *not* refuted by this experiment — we just applied the std
to the wrong probabilities. Next step: keep the deterministic LGBM as
the predictor (the proven 18.18% engine) and use the bootstrap std
purely as a *filter* — bet only when `abs_edge > 0.18 AND ML ≤ +250 AND
bootstrap_std ≤ τ`. This decouples the edge signal from the smoothing
that killed it here.
